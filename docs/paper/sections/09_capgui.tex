\section{CapGui}
\label{sec:capgui}

CapGui is the graphical interface of \CAP{}: it opens the files written by the jobs, draws their histograms, data
points and functions, fits them, and exports the figures. It is built on Dear ImGui~\cite{DearImGui} with its GLFW
and OpenGL back ends, and uses no ROOT class; it reads ROOT files only when \CAP{} is built with ROOT-file support, to
import their histograms. The program is started with the files or folders to open,
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
CapGui [--config file.xml] [file or folder ...]
\end{verbatim}}
\noindent and its settings (theme, layout, fonts, the folders shown at start) are kept in an XML file, read from the
home directory of the user or from the configuration directory of \CAP{}, and written back by the Preferences
dialog.

CapGui draws with the graphics library of \CAP{} (Section~\ref{sec:graphics}): \code{ImGuiPainter} implements the
abstract \code{Painter} for the screen, so that a canvas is drawn by the same code on the screen and, through the SVG
and PDF painters, in the files it exports, and what is seen is what is saved. Labels containing \LaTeX{} (or the
TLatex notation of ROOT, translated first) are typeset by the system \code{latex} and \code{dvisvgm} in a background
thread, cached in memory and on disk, and drawn as textures; plain text is shown until the image is ready, so that
typesetting never blocks the interface.

\subsection{Windows, canvases and graphs}
\label{sec:windows}

\paragraph{Panes and windows.}
The main window holds a menu bar, the data browser, the canvases and a status bar, as panes that can be docked side
by side, stacked as tabs, or dragged out into windows of their own. Canvases are arranged in a grid of $n\times m$
panes, and new ones are added at any time; a canvas holds one or several graphs, arranged in a grid of its own, and
a graph holds any number of objects, overlaid. The Canvases window lists all the canvases, grouped by window.

\paragraph{The data browser.}
The browser shows the files opened (CAP files, Section~\ref{sec:binary}, read lazily, and ROOT files) and the
folders of the file system as trees, in which a CAP file opens when it is expanded; a filter selects objects by name,
and each entry shows the type of the object (one- or two-dimensional histogram, profile, data points) with its title,
binning and number of entries. An object is drawn by clicking on it, which shows it in the selected graph, or by
dragging it onto any graph, either overlaid on what the graph holds or replacing it; a folder can be drawn one object
per graph. Objects can also be copied, by dragging, into trees made in the session, organised in folders, and saved
as a new CAP file; a folder or a file can be exported to a ROOT file. The data table pane lists the points of the
selected object with their coordinates, values and statistical and systematic uncertainties and, when the object has
fits, the values of the fitted functions and the pulls.

\paragraph{Interacting with graphs.}
Dragging a rectangle in a graph zooms on it, dragging along an axis zooms on that axis only, and a double click
returns to the automatic ranges; in the three-dimensional view of a map, dragging rotates it. The title, the axis
titles and the legend are moved by dragging, and a right click on any of them, or on an object, opens its editor:
range, scale, ticks and labels of an axis; text, font, size and colour of a title, with a preview of its \LaTeX{};
position and frame of the legend; markers, lines and the display of the statistical and systematic uncertainties of
an object, or the palette, contours and surface of a map. Shapes and text boxes are added from a tool palette.
Changes can be undone and redone, and the contents of a graph can be cut, copied and pasted into another. The status
bar shows the coordinates under the mouse.

\paragraph{Export.}
A graph, a canvas or the whole window, as it is laid out, is saved as an SVG or PDF file; all the canvases can be
saved at once, or as the pages of one PDF book. With the \LaTeX{} library (Section~\ref{sec:latex}), the canvases
are also written as a Beamer presentation, one frame per canvas or per graph, which can be compiled and opened from
CapGui.

\paragraph{Comparisons.}
Two histograms with the same binning are compared in a graph that overlays them above a panel showing their
difference, together with the statistical tests of Section~\ref{sec:mathother}: the $\chi^2$ test of their
compatibility (of the counts, of the values, or of the shapes) and, in one dimension, the Kolmogorov--Smirnov test.
A two-dimensional histogram is projected onto either axis, with its fits.

\subsection{Fits}
\label{sec:guifits}

The Fit window gives access, without programming, to the fitting library of \CAP{} described in
Section~\ref{sec:fitting}; it is a front end to the same classes that programs use, and a fit made in either is
seen in the other.

\paragraph{Objects and functions.}
Data points, histograms and functions of one or two variables are fitted; a function is sampled on a set of points
or a grid first. The functions offered are those of the registry (Section~\ref{sec:functions}) of the rank of the
object: constant, linear and polynomial functions, Gaussian, log-normal and exponential functions, orthogonal series
(Legendre, Chebyshev, Hermite, Laguerre), Bessel functions, the two-dimensional Gaussian and polynomials, and any
class registered by a program. Composite functions are built in a dialog, either as a sum of registered functions or,
in two variables, as a product $f(x)\,g(y)$ of functions of one variable; their components are labelled, and the
parameters named after them (\code{signal.mean}, \code{background.a1}). A composite is either used for the session or
registered under a name, which keeps it in the file of composites shared by CapGui and the programs. Each parameter
has a starting value and can be fixed or bounded; the starting values can be estimated from the data in the
selection (Section~\ref{sec:fitting}), and after a fit the fitted values become the starting values of the next.

\paragraph{Statistic and method.}
The statistic is the $\chi^2$ or, for histograms, the Poisson likelihood ratio, compared to the function at the centre
of each bin or to its average over the bin. The minimisation is Levenberg--Marquardt, optionally preceded by a global
search by differential evolution over the bounds of the free parameters, for poor starting values or several minima.
The fitting library offers more than the Fit window uses: unbinned and extended likelihoods, the other global
searches of \code{ExtremumSearch} (simulated annealing, genetic algorithm, divide and conquer, explore--exploit), and
Bayesian fits with an adaptive Metropolis--Hastings sampler (Section~\ref{sec:fitting}); these are, for now,
available to programs only.

\paragraph{Ranges and regions.}
The range of each variable is typed in, or taken from the range shown in the graph or from the whole object. Regions
are added as rows that include or exclude an interval of $x$, of $y$, or of both: a band or a box, which leaves out a
peak or a dead area, or selects two sidebands (Section~\ref{sec:fitting}). For a histogram, the limits can snap to
the edges of the bins, and the number of points used is shown as the regions are edited.

\paragraph{Results.}
A fit is attached to the object as a fit record (Section~\ref{sec:fitting}), drawn with it, and saved with it when
the object is written to a CAP file; the fits of a file are thus found again, in CapGui or in a program, with the
object they were made to. The Fits pane, below the canvases, shows one tab per fit of the selected graph: the
quality of the fit (the statistic, the number of degrees of freedom, $\chi^2/\nu$ and the $p$-value, the number of
points used, the convergence and the method), the parameters with their uncertainties and bounds, flagged when one
is at a bound, and the correlation matrix of the free parameters, with the strong correlations ($|\rho|>0.9$)
highlighted. The pane also sets how each fit is drawn (line, colour, over the selection or the whole range), whether
its quality is shown in the legend, and whether a residual panel is drawn below the graph, with the differences
between the data and the fit, or the pulls. For a fit in two variables, the map of the pulls is drawn in a new
canvas.
